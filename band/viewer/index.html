Build order

Everything in the drawing, in the order to do it. Parts from the top, all solder underneath, leads clipped to 1.5 mm. Wires are tinned and laid along a stub, one joint each.

1. Parts

  1. Pico: rows 1–20, columns 2 and 9, USB face flush with the row-1 edge. Tack two diagonal pins, check it sits flat and square, solder the other 38, clip all to 1.5 mm. Plug USB in and run the blink before anything else goes on.
  2. green LED: as soldered: LONG leg (anode) in (22,8), SHORT leg in (21,8). It sits the other way from the first plan; the two connections below are swapped to match, nothing to redo.
  3. 470 Ω: flat on row 21, legs (21,4) and (21,6). Solder, clip. Its (21,6) end reaches the LED's long leg at (22,8) by the L-shaped solder run in section 2.
  4. 1N5817 diode 1: flat on row 22, STRIPE end (22,2), plain end (22,5). Solder, clip.
  5. R1 100k: flat on column 10, legs (4,10) and (7,10). Solder, clip. Its top (4,10) is the switch-output tap; keep that blob off (4,9).
  6. R2 100k: flat on column 10, legs (8,10) and (12,10), right below R1. Solder, clip. Keep the (8,10) blob off (8,9), which is ground: the midpoint bridge in section 2 joins (7,10) to (8,10) vertically, not sideways.
  7. S8050: standing on column 1, legs E (13,1), B (14,1), C (15,1), flat face away from the Pico, E nearest the USB end. Solder, clip.
  8. 1 kΩ: as built: the transistor's base leg was cut short, so the 1 kΩ's lead is soldered to that stub above the board and continues down through (14,1); the other lead is in (18,1). Solder both, clip. (14,1) underneath is the base node: nothing else joins it.

2. Solder bridges underneath

  1. 470 → LED + (L-shaped run): one run of solder across (21,6) – (21,7) – (22,7) – (22,8)
  2. E → GND: one run of solder across (13,1) – (13,2)
  3. 1 k → GP13 (up one, then across; NOT across at row 18, that is ground): one run of solder across (18,1) – (17,1) – (17,2)
  4. divider midpoint: R1 bottom → R2 top: one run of solder across (7,10) – (8,10)
  5. divider mid → GP28: one run of solder across (7,10) – (7,9)
  6. R2 bottom → GND (down one, across one): one run of solder across (12,10) – (13,10) – (13,9)
  7. 3V3 → 3V3 point: one run of solder across (5,9) – (5,10)

3. Wires that stay under the board

  1. VSYS 55 mm: from the stub at (22,2) to the stub at (2,9), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.
  2. divider tap 50 mm: from the stub at (22,5) to the stub at (4,10), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.
  3. LED supply 45 mm: from the stub at (21,4) to the stub at (5,9), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.
  4. LED GND 12 mm: from the stub at (21,8) to the stub at (18,9), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.

4. Leads leaving the board (tin, lay along the stub, one joint)

Lengths are cut lengths: the route inside the box plus 15 mm of handling slack. The long ones are long because the sockets sit at the far end of the box from the pins they feed; the trunk A leads also cross the middle wall. Section 2 draws the socket leads pin by pin.

  2. switch

  3. light bar

    • light bar +5V 55 → stub at (5,9)
    • light bar DIN 90 → stub at (20,9)
  4. trunk A
    • trunk A MOT− 85 (S8050 C leg) → stub at (15,1)
    • trunk A 3V3 + MOT+ 135 each (3V3 point) → stub at (5,10)
    • trunk A EMG2 125 → stub at (9,9)
    • trunk A EMG1 120 → stub at (10,9)
  5. trunk B
    • trunk B SDA 75 → stub at (6,2)
    • trunk B SCL 75 → stub at (7,2)
    • trunk B INT 70 → stub at (9,2)
    • trunk B BTN 65 → stub at (10,2)
  6. cord
    • cord TX 95 → stub at (1,2)
    • cord RX 90 → stub at (2,2)
    • cord GND 50 → stub at (18,2)
  7. VBUS
    • VBUS → diode 3 → IN+ 55 + diode + 15 → stub at (1,9)
    • diode 3 sits in this wire at the CHARGER end, not the middle: 55 mm from the stub to its plain end, the diode lying in the channel beside the Qi board's middle-wall edge (stripe toward the charger, heat-shrink over it), 15 mm from the stripe end to IN+. Stubs that take two wires: (5,9) 3V3 takes the LED supply and the light bar +5V; (8,2) GND takes the light bar GND and trunk A GND; (5,10) takes trunk A 3V3 and MOT+. Tin both ends together, then one joint.

5. Meter checks before it goes in the box

  1. LED: diode mode, red on (22,8) stub, black on (21,8) stub: ~2 V and a faint glow (long leg is in row 22).
  2. 470 Ω: ohms, (21,4) stub to (22,8) stub: 470. Open = the (21,6)–(21,7)–(22,7)–(22,8) run is incomplete.
  3. (21,8) must not touch (22,8) or (22,7): (21,8) is ground, (22,7)/(22,8) are the LED supply; a short there kills the LED and warms the 470.
  4. Diode 1: diode mode, red on (22,5) stub, black on (22,2) stub: ~0.2 V. Swapped = diode in backwards.
  5. Divider: ohms (4,10) to (12,10): 200 k; (7,9) to either end: 100 k; (12,10) to (13,9) near zero. (8,10) to (8,9) must be open.
  6. S8050: diode mode, red on (14,1) stub (the base node): ~0.6 V to E (13,1) and to C (15,1). Open both = legs swapped.
  7. 1 kΩ: ohms (17,2) to (14,1): 1 k. (18,1) to (18,2) must be OPEN, that is ground; if it beeps the motor can never drive.
  8. No bridge (13,1)/(13,2) to (14,1): ohms B to GND reads open (not the 1 k).
  9. GND to 3V3 (any GND stub to (5,9)): must not beep.

Then Kapton over the solder side (standoff holes clear), and the bench script once more with everything dangling.

2. Band box: the wall sockets and their leads to the strip

Three sockets lie on their backs in pockets behind the box's end walls: the PH6 in the battery bay's end wall, the PH4 and the PH3 side by side in the strip bay's. Their pins point into the bays; thirteen leads run from those pins to the strip's solder-side stubs. Everything here is soldered on the bench and dropped in as one harness; nothing is soldered inside the box.

Build order

  1. Pin 1: hold each socket with its lock side (the open side of the shroud that the plug's ramp faces) up and its mouth toward you; pin 1 is the left pin. Dot it with paint. Use the same rule on the module's two sockets and hold both housings of every cable the same way when crimping: then every cable is straight, pin 1 to pin 1, and can never put 3V3 on GND.
  2. Pins: clip all thirteen to 2 mm and tin them. They are 2 mm apart: small blobs, and a meter check between neighbours after soldering.
  3. Leads onto the pins, on the bench, cut to the lengths in the drawing (30 AWG, colour per net). Tug each one.
  4. Other ends onto the strip, board upside down: tin the lead, lay it along the stub, one joint. (5,10) takes two (3V3 and MOT+): tin them together first. (8,2) takes trunk A GND and the light bar GND the same way.
  5. Meter: every pin beeps to its own stub and to nothing else; PH6 pin 1 to pin 2 (3V3 to GND) must be open; PH6 pin 1 to pin 5 beeps (both are the 3V3 point, by design).
  6. Routes once in the box: the PH6 leads cross the middle wall through the notch beside its pocket and go under the strip's +X end; 3V3, MOT+, EMG1 and EMG2 continue under the Pico in the bare lanes to columns 9–10, GND and MOT− stay on the column-1 side. PH4 and PH3 leads go straight under the strip from its +X end along columns 1–2.
  7. Into the box (BENCH 7c order): PH6 into the battery-bay pocket first, before the charger and cell bury it, leads through the notch; charger, Qi, cell, coil; then PH4 (middle-wall side) and PH3 (chest side) into the strip-bay pockets; hot glue on top of each; then the strip onto its standoffs with the tails bunched at the ends.
  8. First plug-in: with the module wired by the same pin-1 rule, meter box PH6 pin 1 to module PH6 pin 1 through the trunk cable (beep) and box pin 1 to module pin 6 (open) before power.

3. Battery bay: cell, charger, switch, Qi

Port to port. Nothing here touches the strip except three wires: the switch return into (22,5), ground into (3,2), and the VBUS lead out of (1,9).

Build order

  1. Pigtail: 2-pin PH header, red 30 mm to B+, black 30 mm to B−, wires in from the top, soldered underneath. Meter the cell's plug to find plus before the first plug-in; red must land on B+.
  2. Charger leads, from the top, soldered underneath: OUT− 55 mm to (3,2); OUT+ 110 mm to the switch; the VBUS lead (55 mm + diode 3 + 15 mm) and the Qi + lead (diode 2 in it the same way) both onto IN+; Qi − onto IN−. Leave the board's own USB-C empty.
  3. Qi receiver: splice diode 2 into its + output wire, stripe toward the charger, heat-shrink; + → IN+, − → IN−. Coil: ferrite sheet on the side that faces the cell.
  4. Diode 3 in the strip's VBUS lead: 55 mm from stub (1,9) to diode 3's plain end; the diode lies in the channel beside the Qi board's middle-wall edge, stripe toward the charger, heat-shrink over it; 15 mm from the stripe end to IN+, twisted with diode 2's stripe end: tin, one joint on the IN+ pad.
  5. Switch: OUT+ to its middle pin (110 mm: through the USB-end notch, along the strip's middle-wall edge, past row 22); its outer pin to the strip's diode 1 plain stub (22,5), 30 mm; the second outer pin stays empty.
  6. Bench proof before the box (BENCH stage 1 + 3): OUT+ to OUT− reads the cell; IN− ↔ OUT− beeps, B− ↔ OUT− does not; switch off + coil on the pad = red LED, Pico dark; switch on = Pico runs; USB in = Pico runs and red LED.
  7. Into the box, in this order: box onto the plate (2 × M2×6 from inside) → trunk A header into the battery-bay end-wall pocket, leads behind the ring → charger into its cradle, USB-C toward the triceps wall, pads toward the middle wall, cell leads back through the ring's notch → Kapton over it → Qi board on the four bracket tops → cell into the ring, lip toward the charger → ferrite + coil on the cell, Kapton band → strip-side headers → strip → switch onto its shelf → light bar → lid.

4. Forearm module: boards, IMU, button, motor, trunk headers

Port to port. Everything inside the module ends on the two stacked headers; the trunk cables carry it to the box.

Build order

  1. IMU: clip its header to ~2 mm; one short jumper joins PS0, PS1 and AD0 to the board's own GND pin (I²C mode, address 0x4A; the firmware probes both), another joins RST to VCC; leave CS open. Five 60 mm wires on VCC, GND, SDA, SCL, INT. Screw it to its two posts in bay B, header edge toward the rib, 2 × M2×4.
  2. Headers: clip the PH6's and PH4's pins to 2 mm, solder a 60 mm lead on every pin, drop both into the elbow-wall pocket, PH6 below (trunk A), PH4 above (trunk B), hot glue on top. Pin 1 by the same rule as in the box (section 2): lock side up, mouth toward you, pin 1 on the left; then the trunk cables are straight, 1 to 1.
  3. Motor: solder the 1N4007 across its two tabs first, standing up along the tab slot, stripe on the tab that will be MOT+ (PH6 pin 5); the other tab is MOT− (PH6 pin 6). Leads up the tab slot into the rib tunnel and out the wall hole into bay B; peel the pad, press it onto the pocket floor, tab toward the button.
  4. Button: clip two legs flush and the other two to 1.5 mm; two wires soldered sideways (one leg → PH4 pin 4, the diagonal leg → PH6 pin 2); down the tunnel with the motor leads; drop it in its pocket cap up.
  5. Board A (flexor, EMG1): three wires on its Gravity pins under the board (− → PH6 2, + → PH6 1, S → PH6 3), pass them through the rib passage into bay B, screw the board to its posts, jack end to the wrist wall, 2 × M3×5.
  6. Board B (extensor, EMG2): same, − → PH6 2, + → PH6 1, S → PH6 4; it sits over the IMU on the tall posts, 2 × M3×6.
  7. Header pins with many wires: PH6 pin 1 takes three (board A +, board B +, IMU VCC), pin 2 takes four (board A −, board B −, IMU GND, button). Twist and tin each group, then one joint on the pin.
  8. Close: backer under the strap with the strap between its rails, module on top, 2 × M2×8 from the skin side into the inserts under the crest bosses (pan heads), lid on, 4 × M2×6 (crest and side ears). Bench: plug the trunk into the box, run the stage-4 script; IMU ok and both EMG columns move.
  9. Electrode frames: plate into the frame bars down, loop band down through one cover slot and up the other, cover on, 4 × M2×6; 3.5 mm cable to the matching jack (flexor → board A, extensor → board B).

5. The whole band and its cables

Where each box sits and which plug joins them. The band never receives commands; the cables carry power and signals out to the forearm, and serial to the hand.

Cables to make

  1. Trunk A: PH6 housing at both ends, six wires 25 cm, order 3V3 GND EMG1 EMG2 MOT+ MOT− pin 1 to 6 at both ends (straight through). Twist EMG1, EMG2 and GND together along the run; twist MOT+ with MOT−.
  2. Trunk B: PH4 both ends, SDA SCL INT BTN straight through, 25 cm. Glue its module-end housing face-to-face with trunk A's after a first plug-in so the pair sits square in the stacked pocket.
  3. Hand cord: PH3 both ends, TX RX GND, crossed (band TX → hand RX). Make it when the claw is ready.
  4. Plate cables: the kit's 3.5 mm leads, flexor plate → jack A, extensor plate → jack B.
  5. Crimp recipe (PA-09): strip 2.5 mm, no tinning, conductor in the 1.4 slot, insulation in the 1.9 slot, tug test, contact in with its lance toward the housing windows until it clicks. Thin wire: double the copper over, heat-shrink under the insulation tabs.

The slide switch is not on the strip: it sits on a shelf on the chest wall (right-hand side here) just past row 22, between the LED and the corner lid boss, and reaches the board only through its two wires, charger OUT+ into its middle pin and its outer pin down to diode 1 at (22,5). Every hole in columns 1 and 10 has a Pico pin 2.5 mm away: keep those joints small. Column 10 stays flat (the LED hole is over rows 17–20). Underside wires cross in the bare lanes under the Pico, never over a stub. Source of truth: band/PARTS.md, “Strip map”.
