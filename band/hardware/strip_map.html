Band build maps

Four separate sections: the strip (both sides), the battery bay, the forearm module, and the whole band with its cables. Each has its own diagram and its own build order; nothing in one section refers to holes in another.

Build order

Everything in the drawing, in the order to do it. Parts from the top, all solder underneath, leads clipped to 1.5 mm. Wires are tinned and laid along a stub, one joint each.

1. Parts

  1. Pico: rows 1–20, columns 2 and 9, USB face flush with the row-1 edge. Tack two diagonal pins, check it sits flat and square, solder the other 38, clip all to 1.5 mm. Plug USB in and run the blink before anything else goes on.
  2. green LED: long leg (21,8), short leg (22,8). Hold the flange 1 mm above the board while soldering. Clip.
  3. 470 Ω: flat on row 21, legs (21,4) and (21,6). Solder, clip.
  4. 1N5817 diode 1: flat on row 22, STRIPE end (22,2), plain end (22,5). Solder, clip.
  5. R1 100k: flat on column 10, legs (4,10) and (7,10). Solder (4,10) only for now, clip it.
  6. R2 100k: flat on column 10, legs (7,10) and (10,10). Its top leg goes into (7,10) WITH R1's leg: twist the two together first. Solder (7,10) and (10,10), clip.
  7. S8050: standing on column 1, legs E (13,1), B (14,1), C (15,1), flat face away from the Pico, E nearest the USB end. Solder, clip.
  8. 1 kΩ: one lead soldered to the transistor's B leg 3 mm above the board (a top-side joint), body lying toward the far end on the outer side of the C leg, other lead into (17,1). Solder (17,1), clip.

2. Solder bridges underneath

  1. 470 → LED +: one run of solder across (21,6) – (21,7) – (21,8)
  2. E → GND: one run of solder across (13,1) – (13,2)
  3. 1 k → GP13: one run of solder across (17,1) – (17,2)
  4. divider mid → GP28: one run of solder across (7,10) – (7,9)
  5. 3V3 → 3V3 point: one run of solder across (5,9) – (5,10)

3. Wires that stay under the board

  1. VSYS 55 mm: from the stub at (22,2) to the stub at (2,9), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.
  2. divider tap 50 mm: from the stub at (22,5) to the stub at (4,10), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.
  3. divider GND 10 mm: from the stub at (10,10) to the stub at (8,9), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.
  4. LED supply 45 mm: from the stub at (21,4) to the stub at (5,9), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.
  5. LED GND 12 mm: from the stub at (22,8) to the stub at (18,9), routed in the bare lanes, never over another stub.

4. Leads leaving the board (tin, lay along the stub, one joint)

  1. charger
    • charger OUT− 60 → stub at (3,2)
    • charger OUT+ 60 → the switch's middle pin (not the strip)
  2. switch
    • switch outer 60 → stub at (22,5)
  3. light bar
    • light bar GND 60 + trunk A GND 60 → stub at (8,2)
    • light bar +5V 60 → stub at (5,9)
    • light bar DIN 60 → stub at (20,9)
  4. trunk A
    • trunk A MOT− 60 (S8050 C leg) → stub at (15,1)
    • trunk A 3V3 + MOT+ 60 each (3V3 point) → stub at (5,10)
    • trunk A EMG2 60 → stub at (9,9)
    • trunk A EMG1 60 → stub at (10,9)
  5. trunk B
    • trunk B SDA 60 → stub at (6,2)
    • trunk B SCL 60 → stub at (7,2)
    • trunk B INT 60 → stub at (9,2)
    • trunk B BTN 60 → stub at (10,2)
  6. cord
    • cord TX 60 → stub at (1,2)
    • cord RX 60 → stub at (2,2)
    • cord GND 60 → stub at (18,2)
  7. VBUS
    • VBUS → 1N5817 → charger IN+ 60 → stub at (1,9)
    • the 1N5817 sits in this wire, stripe toward the charger; heat-shrink over it

Stubs that take two wires: (5,9) 3V3 takes the LED supply and the light bar +5V; (8,2) GND takes the light bar GND and trunk A GND; (5,10) takes trunk A 3V3 and MOT+. Tin both ends together, then one joint.

5. Meter checks before it goes in the box

  1. LED: diode mode, red on (21,8) stub, black on (22,8) stub: ~2 V and a faint glow. Open = LED backwards.
  2. 470 Ω: ohms, (21,4) stub to (21,8) stub: 470. Open = the (21,6)–(21,8) run is incomplete.
  3. (22,8) must not touch (21,8): diode mode both ways between them reads open in one direction, not a short.
  4. Diode 1: diode mode, red on (22,5) stub, black on (22,2) stub: ~0.2 V. Swapped = diode in backwards.
  5. Divider: ohms (4,10) to (10,10): 200 k; (7,9) to either end: 100 k.
  6. S8050: diode mode, red on B (14,1): ~0.6 V to E and to C. Open both = legs swapped.
  7. No bridge (13,1)/(13,2) to (14,1): ohms B to GND reads open (not the 1 k).
  8. GND to 3V3 (any GND stub to (5,9)): must not beep.

Then Kapton over the solder side (standoff holes clear), and the bench script once more with everything dangling.

2. Battery bay: cell, charger, switch, Qi

Port to port. Nothing here touches the strip except three wires: the switch return into (22,5), ground into (3,2), and the VBUS lead out of (1,9).

Build order

  1. Pigtail: 2-pin PH header, red 30 mm to B+, black 30 mm to B−, wires in from the top, soldered underneath. Meter the cell's plug to find plus before the first plug-in; red must land on B+.
  2. Charger leads, 60 mm, from the top, soldered underneath: OUT+, OUT−, IN+, IN−. Leave the board's own USB-C empty.
  3. Qi receiver: splice diode 2 into its + output wire, stripe toward the charger, heat-shrink; + → IN+, − → IN−. Coil: ferrite sheet on the side that faces the cell.
  4. Diode 3 in the strip's VBUS lead (from stub (1,9)), stripe toward the charger, joins IN+ with diode 2's wire: twist, tin, one joint on the IN+ pad.
  5. Switch: OUT+ to its middle pin; its outer pin to the strip's diode 1 plain stub (22,5); the second outer pin stays empty.
  6. Bench proof before the box (BENCH stage 1 + 3): OUT+ to OUT− reads the cell; IN− ↔ OUT− beeps, B− ↔ OUT− does not; switch off + coil on the pad = red LED, Pico dark; switch on = Pico runs; USB in = Pico runs and red LED.
  7. Into the box, in this order: box onto the plate (2 × M2×6 from inside) → trunk A header into the battery-bay end-wall pocket, leads behind the ring → charger into its cradle, USB-C toward the triceps wall, pads toward the middle wall, cell leads back through the ring's notch → Kapton over it → Qi board on the four bracket tops → cell into the ring, lip toward the charger → ferrite + coil on the cell, Kapton band → strip-side headers → strip → switch between its fins → light bar → lid.

3. Forearm module: boards, IMU, button, motor, trunk headers

Port to port. Everything inside the module ends on the two stacked headers; the trunk cables carry it to the box.

Build order

  1. IMU: clip its header to ~2 mm; one short jumper joins PS0, PS1 and AD0 to the board's own GND pin (I²C mode, address 0x4A; the firmware probes both), another joins RST to VCC; leave CS open. Five 60 mm wires on VCC, GND, SDA, SCL, INT. Screw it to its two posts in bay B, header edge toward the rib, 2 × M2×4.
  2. Headers: clip the PH6's and PH4's pins to 2 mm, solder a 60 mm lead on every pin, drop both into the elbow-wall pocket, PH6 below (trunk A), PH4 above (trunk B), hot glue on top. Pin 1 is the end with the housing's ramp.
  3. Motor: solder the 1N4007 across its two tabs first, standing up along the tab slot, stripe on the tab that will be MOT+ (PH6 pin 5); the other tab is MOT− (PH6 pin 6). Leads up the tab slot into the rib tunnel and out the wall hole into bay B; peel the pad, press it onto the pocket floor, tab toward the button.
  4. Button: clip two legs flush and the other two to 1.5 mm; two wires soldered sideways (one leg → PH4 pin 4, the diagonal leg → PH6 pin 2); down the tunnel with the motor leads; drop it in its pocket cap up.
  5. Board A (flexor, EMG1): three wires on its Gravity pins under the board (− → PH6 2, + → PH6 1, S → PH6 3), pass them through the rib passage into bay B, screw the board to its posts, jack end to the wrist wall, 2 × M3×5.
  6. Board B (extensor, EMG2): same, − → PH6 2, + → PH6 1, S → PH6 4; it sits over the IMU on the tall posts, 2 × M3×6.
  7. Header pins with many wires: PH6 pin 1 takes three (board A +, board B +, IMU VCC), pin 2 takes four (board A −, board B −, IMU GND, button). Twist and tin each group, then one joint on the pin.
  8. Close: backer under the strap with the strap between its rails, module on top, 2 × M2×12 from the skin side (pan heads), lid on, 2 × M2×4. Bench: plug the trunk into the box, run the stage-4 script; IMU ok and both EMG columns move.
  9. Electrode frames: plate into the frame bars down, loop band down through one cover slot and up the other, cover on, 4 × M2×6; 3.5 mm cable to the matching jack (flexor → board A, extensor → board B).

4. The whole band and its cables

Where each box sits and which plug joins them. The band never receives commands; the cables carry power and signals out to the forearm, and serial to the hand.

Cables to make

  1. Trunk A: PH6 housing at both ends, six wires 25 cm, order 3V3 GND EMG1 EMG2 MOT+ MOT− pin 1 to 6 at both ends (straight through). Twist EMG1, EMG2 and GND together along the run; twist MOT+ with MOT−.
  2. Trunk B: PH4 both ends, SDA SCL INT BTN straight through, 25 cm. Glue its module-end housing face-to-face with trunk A's after a first plug-in so the pair sits square in the stacked pocket.
  3. Hand cord: PH3 both ends, TX RX GND, crossed (band TX → hand RX). Make it when the claw is ready.
  4. Plate cables: the kit's 3.5 mm leads, flexor plate → jack A, extensor plate → jack B.
  5. Crimp recipe (PA-09): strip 2.5 mm, no tinning, conductor in the 1.4 slot, insulation in the 1.9 slot, tug test, contact in with its lance toward the housing windows until it clicks. Thin wire: double the copper over, heat-shrink under the insulation tabs.

The slide switch is not on the strip: it is glued between the two fins on the chest wall (right-hand side here, beside rows 12–17) and reaches the board only through its two wires, charger OUT+ into its middle pin and its outer pin down to diode 1 at (22,5). Every hole in columns 1 and 10 has a Pico pin 2.5 mm away: keep those joints small. Column 10 above row 10 stays empty (switch fins rows 12–17, LED hole).
